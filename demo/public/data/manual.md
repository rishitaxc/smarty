# Northwind maintenance guide (illustrative, written for this demo)

## 4.1 Gearbox condition indicators
Drive-train acceleration (vibration) and gear oil temperature are compared with the turbine's own 30-day baseline and with the fleet median for the same hours. Wind and load changes affect every turbine together, so a turbine that drifts away from the fleet is a stronger signal than an absolute value.

## 4.2 High-speed shaft (HSS) bearing wear
Advanced HSS bearing wear is indicated when BOTH are true:
- drive-train vibration above 2x the turbine's own baseline, or above 2.5x the fleet median over 48 hours;
- gear oil temperature more than 5 °C above the fleet median over the last 10 days.
Typical remaining life once both thresholds are met: 7 to 14 days at normal load. Earlier oil samples with rising particle counts support the diagnosis.

## 4.3 HSS bearing replacement (uptower)
- No crane required. Turbine stopped, rotor locked, lock-out/tag-out applied.
- Crew: 2 gearbox-certified, climb-certified technicians. Duration: 6 hours.
- Hub-height wind must stay below 10 m/s for the whole window; schedule in the lowest forecast wind to minimise lost energy.
- Consumables: 1 x SEAL-HSS-KIT, 2 x OIL-GB-320, 1 x FLT-GB-OFF.

## 4.4 Consequence of failure in service
A seized HSS bearing can damage the gear stages. Gearbox exchange then needs a crane and a replacement gearbox; outages of 4 to 8 weeks are typical.

## 7.2 Chilled-water pump mechanical seal
Seal failure is indicated when seal-chamber temperature exceeds 50 °C and pump vibration exceeds 4.5 mm/s, often with falling flow. Replace the mechanical seal within 7 days. Crew: 1 HVAC technician, 3 hours, pump isolated. Schedule when cooling demand is lowest (night) and the standby pump is running. Consumables: none beyond the seal kit.
